# CEO For A Day: the course map

Status: **draft for Atheeb's review**, 30 Oct 2026 (revised after a fresh-context review). It builds on
`docs/LEARNING-PLAN.md` (approved) and `docs/research/curriculum.md` (the first syllabus). Where this file
and the research draft disagree, this file wins.

**Totals:** 13 units, 34 modules, 138 lessons, 24 Hot Seat gates (14 we have and 10 new ones), plus 1 backup
Hot Seat and 1 existing Hot Seat as optional practice.

---

## 1. How to read this

- **Codes.** A lesson is `U.M.L` (unit, module, lesson). Module `4.2` is the second module of Unit 4.
- **One lesson, one rule.** The rule column is what the player keeps: 12 words or fewer, simple English. It
  becomes the takeaway card and the flashcard.
- **Examples are made up.** Every lesson example is a made-up Indian small business (Raju's kirana, Lata's
  tiffin service, a tailor). **No lesson names a real company.** Real companies appear only in Hot Seats and
  their debriefs, so a lesson never gives away a gate's answer, and lessons need no fact-check.
- **Source.** The book, course, law or page the rule comes from. ✓ = a source we opened (in a research file
  or `docs/research/curriculum.md`). ◇ = from general knowledge; check it before the lesson ships.
- **Exercise.** The best main exercise type for the rule, from the 12 in `learning-design.md`. Every lesson
  still mixes 4 or more types.
- **Decision → lessons.** For each Hot Seat decision, the first code is the main lesson it tests. Codes after
  "+" are lessons it also touches. In the game, each choice's "why" names the main lesson.

### Gates, Practice nodes and test-out

- **A gate** is a Hot Seat at the end of a module. The next module unlocks when the gate is finished (any
  score, even "fired"). Atheeb said a module *can* have several gates, not that it must have one, and the plan
  says a Hot Seat about every two modules. So **one gate can close two modules**; it sits after the second.
  Some modules have **no gate**: they end with the unit's Practice node (Daily Calls are suspended for now).
  Each module below says which.
- **Where a Hot Seat goes:** the first module where most of its decisions' main lessons have been taught,
  **and** its title decision has been taught too. The second half matters for Tanishq, New Coke, Netflix and
  Intel: by count alone they would land before the call they are named for.
- **Test-out.** A confident player can open a gate early. A good call on a decision passes **only the lessons
  that decision tests**. Every other lesson in the modules the gate closes gets a short test-out quiz: one card
  per lesson. Gateless modules are tested out by the quiz alone.

### Path order of the units, and why

| # | Unit | Lessons | Why it sits here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Make money, keep money | 15 | Profit, cash and udhaar: what every owner meets on Monday. "Judge the thinking, not the luck" is here because every Hot Seat's feedback works that way. |
| 2 | Customers first | 9 | Find a real problem and the right buyer before pricing anything. |
| 3 | Price and offer | 9 | Price comes straight after knowing what the customer values. |
| 4 | Trust and brand | 10 | In India, trust is often the product. |
| 5 | Getting customers | 10 | **New.** Selling step by step, channels, and what one customer is worth. |
| 6 | Running the business | 7 | Once there are sales: keep it simple, get good suppliers. |
| 7 | People | 10 | Hiring, handing over, family partners, letting people go, leading in bad times. |
| 8 | Rivals, partners and deals | 14 | Who holds power, cheaper rivals, thinking one move ahead, and **negotiation (new)**. |
| 9 | Money: numbers, funding and rules | 18 | Read the numbers, raise money (including Indian small-business loans), survive a slump, set up the business legally, and stay clean. |
| 10 | Strategy | 10 | Rumelt and Playing to Win, once the player has met the problems they solve. Growing big safely. |
| 11 | What keeps rivals out | 9 | 7 Powers ("moats"), plus raising prices as the test. |
| 12 | When the world changes | 10 | Disruption. Late, because it needs strategy first. |
| 13 | Crises and big bets | 7 | The hardest calls: recalls and buying companies. |

Cash and customers come before frameworks. Debt, firing and scandal come after the player is hooked. Money
and people sit mid-course (Units 7 and 9), because struggling founders need them sooner than disruption.

### The first minutes

1. Three welcome questions.
2. Lesson **1.1.1** (sales are not profit).
3. **kirana-year** (complexity 1) as a taster: onboarding shows its first two calls, as it does today with
   the trial. They need only 1.1.1 and common sense. It is a made-up story, so it needs no company
   fact-check and can be written first. The full run is the Module 1.3 gate.
4. The first gate on the path is **nirma-washing-powder** (complexity 1), after Module 1.2.

So the first two Hot Seats a player meets are both complexity 1, both Indian, and neither is Kodak. Kodak
is gate 21 of 24.
