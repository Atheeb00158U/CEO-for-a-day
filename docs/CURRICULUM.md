Status: **draft for Atheeb's review**, revised 1 Nov 2026 after Atheeb played the first lesson ("no basic
lessons"; see his decisions at the end). It builds on `docs/LEARNING-PLAN.md` (approved) and
`docs/research/curriculum.md` (the first syllabus). Where this file and the research draft disagree, this file

## 1. How to read this

- **Codes.** A lesson is `U.M.L` (unit, module, lesson). Module `4.2` is the second module of Unit 4.
- **One lesson, one rule.** The rule column is what the player keeps: 12 words or fewer, simple English. It
  becomes the takeaway card and the flashcard.
- **Level (Atheeb, 1 Nov 2026: "too basic").** Players already know "profit = sales minus costs". Every
  lesson teaches something a beginner gets wrong, opens with a surprising real case, and asks for real
  working-out (two or three steps, realistic Indian numbers). Fewer, denser lessons beat many easy ones.
- **Basics are asides, never lessons.** Anything a school-leaver already knows (what profit, margin or fixed
  costs are, "keep shop and home money apart", "tax is a cost") is said in one or two conversational lines
  inside a harder lesson: "Sales are all the money that comes in; profit is what's left." Each module lists
  its asides under **Folded in**, with the lesson and the line.
- **Real examples are welcome.** A lesson may open with a famous real Indian company or well-known incident,
  with facts from a source we opened, as long as that company is **not a Hot Seat gate** (so no lesson gives
  away a gate's answer). Practice questions can still use made-up shops.
- **Source.** The book, course, law or page the rule comes from. ✓ = a source we opened (in a research file
  or `docs/research/curriculum.md`). ◇ = from general knowledge; check it before the lesson ships.
- **Exercise.** The best main exercise type for the rule, from the 12 in `learning-design.md`. Every lesson
  still mixes 4 or more types.
- **Decision → lessons.** For each Hot Seat decision, the first code is the main lesson it tests. Codes after
  "+" are lessons it also touches. In the game, each choice's "why" names the main lesson. Decision ids match
  the Hot Seat files (`d1`, `d2`, …) as drafted on 1 Nov 2026.

### Gates, Practice nodes and test-out

- **A gate** is a Hot Seat at the end of a module. The next module unlocks when the gate is finished (any
  score, even "fired"). Atheeb said a module *can* have several gates, not that it must have one, and the plan
  says a Hot Seat about every two modules. So **one gate can close two modules**; it sits after the second.
  Some modules have **no gate**: they end with the unit's Practice node (Daily Calls are suspended for now).
  Each module below says which.
- **Where a Hot Seat goes:** the first module where most of its decisions' main lessons have been taught,
  **and** its title decision has been taught too. The second half matters for Tanishq, New Coke, Netflix and
  Intel: by count alone they would land before the call they are named for.
- **Test-out.** A confident player can open a gate early. A good call on a decision passes **only the lessons
  that decision tests**. Every other lesson in the modules the gate closes gets a short test-out quiz: one card
  per lesson. Gateless modules are tested out by the quiz alone.

### Path order of the units, and why

| # | Unit | Lessons | Why it sits here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Make money, keep money | 8 | The things that kill businesses that look fine: cash running out, hidden costs, money stuck in stock and udhaar, and fixed costs taken on too early. "Judge the thinking, not the luck" is here because every Hot Seat's feedback works that way. |
| 2 | Customers first | 7 | Find a real problem and the right buyer before pricing anything. |
| 3 | Price and offer | 7 | Price comes straight after knowing what the customer values. |
| 4 | Trust and brand | 7 | In India, trust is often the product. |
| 5 | Getting customers | 8 | Selling step by step, channels, and what one customer is worth. |
| 6 | Running the business | 6 | Once there are sales: keep it simple, get good suppliers. |
| 7 | People | 9 | Hiring, handing over, family partners, letting people go, leading in bad times. |
| 8 | Rivals, partners and deals | 12 | Who holds power, cheaper rivals, thinking one move ahead, and negotiation. |
| 9 | Money: numbers, funding and rules | 12 | Read the numbers, raise money (including Indian small-business loans), survive a slump, set up the business legally, and stay clean. |
| 10 | Strategy | 9 | Rumelt and Playing to Win, once the player has met the problems they solve. Growing big safely. |
| 11 | What keeps rivals out | 9 | 7 Powers ("moats"), plus raising prices as the test. |
| 12 | When the world changes | 9 | Disruption. Late, because it needs strategy first. |
| 13 | Crises and big bets | 6 | The hardest calls: recalls and buying companies. |

Cash and customers come before frameworks. Debt, firing and scandal come after the player is hooked. Money
and people sit mid-course (Units 7 and 9), because struggling founders need them sooner than disruption.

## 2. The course

### Unit 1: Make money, keep money

Every Unit 1 lesson opens with a famous real Indian case (none is a gate company), then practises on made-up
shops.

#### Module 1.1: Where the money goes
**Goal:** see why busy, "profitable" shops still run out of money.

| Code | The rule | Opens with (real case) | Practice example (made up) | Source | Exercise |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1.1 | Businesses die when cash runs out, not when profit does. | Jet Airways, one of India's biggest airlines, stopped flying in April 2019, weeks after it ran short of cash for fuel and leases ◇. | Sunil's books show ₹40,000 profit in March, but his buyer pays in 90 days and the cloth mill wants cash today. How many weeks until the box is empty? | *Financial Intelligence for Entrepreneurs* (Berman & Knight) ◇; Paul Graham, "Default Alive or Default Dead?" (2015) ◇ | Spot the mistake (a ledger), then quick sum |
| 1.1.2 | Price minus stock cost isn't profit. Count every hidden cost. | PepperTap, a funded grocery-delivery app, shut in 2016: delivery and discounts cost more than many orders earned ◇. | A ₹400 home-delivery order keeps ₹60 after stock. Take off ₹35 delivery, ₹8 packing, ₹12 spoilt stock: ₹5 left. | Unit economics (YC Startup School) ◇; contribution margin, any cost-accounting text ◇ | Quick sum |
| 1.1.3 | Stock and udhaar are cash you can't spend. Give both limits. | India now makes big buyers pay small suppliers within 45 days (Income Tax s.43B(h), from FY 2023-24), because so much small-business money was stuck in late payments ◇. | A tailor's regulars owe ₹30,000 and ₹50,000 of cloth sits unsold. | 1.1.4 | Judge a call by what you knew then, not the result. | Dhoni gave Joginder Sharma the last over of the 2007 T20 World Cup final. It worked and was praised. Would the same call be "stupid" if it had failed ◇? | A juice stall stocked up for a heatwave that came a week late. The bet was still sound. | *Thinking in Bets* (Annie Duke), "resulting" (sachinrekhi.com) ✓ | True or false, then why |
| 1.1.5 | Test small first, and decide beforehand what result means yes. | Flipkart sold only books when it began in 2007, before it added other goods ◇. | Try home delivery in one lane for a month. |---|---|---|---|---|---|

### Atheeb's decision (1 Nov 2026): no basic lessons

After playing the first lesson: **skip all basic lessons.** Profit, loss and margins are learned at school and
are common sense; if a lesson is too basic, players think "I already know this, this app is wasting my time".
Never teach a basic as its own lesson or module. Fold it into a more detailed lesson as one or two
conversational lines ("Sales are all the money that comes in; profit is what's left"). Every lesson must
teach something a beginner gets wrong, and the very first lesson must make a would-be founder think "I didn't
know that".

Taken as defaults unless Atheeb says otherwise: the kirana story is the onboarding taster; Nokia is optional
practice and Titan quartz the Unit 12 gate; the title-decision placement rule stands; Airbnb 2020 is the
fallback if Zomato's facts are thin; the course stays hidden until kirana, Nirma and redBus are written;
"struggling" players are offered Module 9.2 early; the video pilot story is chosen after the Hot Seats are
planned.
