## Timeline
- 1886: The drink is first made [7]; its taste stays the same for 99 years [2][1].
- After WWII: Coca-Cola has about 60% of the cola market [6].
- 1975: Pepsi starts the "Pepsi Challenge", a blind taste test in which more people pick Pepsi [3].
- 1970s–1984: Coca-Cola's share lead over its main rival slips for 15 years in a row [1]; by 1983 its share is under 24% [6].
- 1982: Coca-Cola launches Diet Coke [3]. While developing it, secret tests find a sweeter formula that beats both Pepsi and the old Coke [2].
- 1983: Caffeine-free Coke and Diet Coke launch [3].
- 1984–1985: Secret "Project Kansas" develops the new formula [6]. The new taste is chosen after tests with nearly 200,000 consumers [1] (History.com says 190,000 [2]). About 10–12% of testers say they feel angry at the idea of change and might stop drinking Coke [6].
- 1985-04-23: Coca-Cola announces it is changing the formula for the first time in 99 years [1], at an event at Lincoln Center, New York [2]. The old recipe is taken off the market [2].
- 1985-04: Coca-Cola's shares fall and Pepsi's rise; Pepsi gives staff a day off and runs full-page ads [2]. Pepsi's sales that month are 14% above the same month a year earlier [6].
- 1985-05/06: Hotline calls rise to 1,500 a day from 400 before the change [1] (History.com reports 5,000 a day, rising to 8,000 by June [2]). About 40,000 letters arrive [4][7]. Protest groups form, one claiming 100,000 members [1]; people hoard the old Coke [1]. A poll finds only 13% of soda drinkers like the new taste [5].
- 1985-07-11: 79 days after the change, the company says sorry and brings back the old recipe as "Coca-Cola Classic" [1][2]. TV networks break into regular shows to report it [2]. The hotline gets 31,600 calls in two days [1].
- 1985: The two drinks get separate ad campaigns, "Red, White and You" for Classic and "Catch the Wave" for new Coke [1].
- Late 1985: Classic outsells new Coke and, within a few months, is again the top sugar cola, ahead of Pepsi [2]; by the end of 1985 it is selling well ahead of both [6]. New Coke falls to about 3% of the market [6].
- 1990: New Coke is renamed Coke II [2][6] (one History.com article says 1992 [3]).
- 2002: Coke II is dropped [2][6].
- 2009: The word "Classic" is removed from Coca-Cola labels in North America [6].

## Decision points
### D1 · 1984 · Why are we losing to Pepsi?
- Situation: Coke's share lead had slipped for 15 years [1]; Pepsi won blind taste tests in the Pepsi Challenge [3], and Coke's own tests agreed [2][3].
- Options a CEO realistically had (3, incl. what the company did): (a) build the brand with new products and ads, keep the recipe (Diet Coke had just shown this could work [3]); (b) cut prices to win buyers back; (c) develop a sweeter new recipe in secret.
- What the company did: it decided taste was the problem and developed a new, sweeter formula in "Project Kansas" [2][6].
- What happened next: the new formula beat both Pepsi and old Coke in tests with nearly 200,000 people [1][2].

### D2 · 1985 · Replace the old Coke?
- Situation: the new taste won the blind tests [1], but the tests did not measure people's bond with the original [1]; about 10–12% of testers were angry at the idea of change [6].
- Options: (a) replace the old Coke with the new one; (b) launch the new taste as an extra drink next to the old one; (c) test it in a few cities first.
- What the company did: it replaced the 99-year-old recipe and took the old one off the market on April 23, 1985 [1][2].
- What happened next: shares fell, Pepsi celebrated [2], and Pepsi's sales jumped 14% that month [6].

### D3 · 1985 · The backlash
- Situation: hotline calls up from 400 to 1,500 a day by June [1] (or 8,000 a day [2]), about 40,000 letters [4], protest groups and hoarding [1], a 13% approval poll [5].
- Options: (a) wait for people to get used to it; (b) spend big on ads for the new taste; (c) bring back the old recipe and say sorry.
- What the company did: on July 11, 1985, after 79 days, it brought back the original as "Coca-Cola Classic" and apologised [1][2].
- What happened next: 31,600 calls in two days [1]; TV news broke in to report it [2].

### D4 · 1985 · Two Cokes on one shelf
- Situation: Classic and the new Coke were both on sale [8].
- Options: (a) make Classic the star and let the new drink fade; (b) keep both, each with its own ads; (c) push the new Coke as the main brand.
- What the company did: it kept both, with separate ad campaigns [1]; the new drink kept the name "Coca-Cola" for a while [3].
- What happened next: Classic outsold the new drink and Pepsi within months [2][6]; New Coke fell to about 3% share [6], became Coke II in 1990 and was dropped in 2002 [2][6].

## What really happened
Coca-Cola changed its 99-year-old recipe on April 23, 1985, after tests with nearly 200,000 people said the sweeter taste won [1]. Fans revolted, and 79 days later the old recipe came back as Coca-Cola Classic [1]. Classic quickly became the top sugar cola again, ahead of Pepsi [2], while the new drink shrank to about 3% of the market [6] and was dropped in 2002 as Coke II [2]. History.com says the company came out of the mess with a stronger market position [2].

## People (by role)
- Chairman and CEO in 1985: Roberto Goizueta (died 1997) [1][2].
- President in 1985: Donald Keough (died 2015), announced the return of the original [2][5].
- Project Kansas leads: Sergio Zyman (marketing) and Brian Dyson [6].
- PepsiCo executive in 1985: Roger Enrico (died 2016) [5][6].
- Protest leader: Gay Mullins, Old Cola Drinkers of America [5][6].
