## Timeline
- 1945: The Government of Bombay starts the Bombay Milk Scheme; milk travels 427 km from Anand to Bombay and must be pasteurised in Anand [1].
- 1945: The government contracts Polsons Limited to supply milk from Anand to Bombay; no one fixes the price paid to farmers, and contractors set it [1]. Polson has an effective monopoly on milk collection in Kaira [3].
- 1946-01-04: At a meeting in Samarkha village, farmers resolve to form a village society in each village and a Union that owns the processing plant, and to refuse to sell to contractors if the government says no [1].
- 1946: The government refuses; the farmers' milk strike lasts 15 days; no milk reaches Bombay from Anand and the Bombay Milk Scheme almost collapses; the milk commissioner accepts the demand [1].
- 1946-12-14: The Kaira District Co-operative Milk Producers' Union is registered [1][3].
- 1948-06: The Union starts pasteurising milk for the Bombay Milk Scheme: two village societies, about 250 litres a day [1].
- 1948 (end): 432 farmers have joined village societies; the Union handles 5,000 litres a day [1].
- 1949: A young engineer, later the Union's general manager, comes to Anand [5].
- 1953: In winter buffaloes give about 2.5 times their summer milk; the Bombay Milk Scheme cannot take the extra, and farmers again sell surplus cheap to middlemen [1]. The dairy declares "milk holidays" [2].
- 1954-11-15: The President of India lays the foundation stone of a Rs 50 lakh milk powder and butter factory, helped by UNICEF, New Zealand (Colombo Plan) and FAO [1].
- 1955: The world's first buffalo milk spray-dryer is installed at Anand; experts had said buffalo milk could not be spray-dried [2][7].
- 1955-10-31: The Prime Minister opens the factory; products are made from buffalo milk for the first time in the world [1].
- 1955: The Union starts selling under the brand "Amul", from the Sanskrit "Amulya", meaning priceless [1][3].
- Later 1950s–60s: Amul makes cheese and baby food from buffalo milk [1][2].
- 1964-10-31: The Prime Minister visits Anand to open a cattle feed plant, stays a night with farmers, and wants the model copied across India [1].
- 1965: The National Dairy Development Board (NDDB) is set up at Anand [1][10].
- 1966: Amul hires an ad agency head to make a campaign for Amul Butter; the little Amul girl and the first topical ads appear [3][8][9].
- 1970-01-13: Operation Flood launches; Phase I is paid for with donated skim milk powder and butter oil, and links 18 milk sheds to Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata and Chennai [4].
- 1973: District unions form the Gujarat Co-operative Milk Marketing Federation (GCMMF) to market together, cut ad costs and stop competing; Kaira gives it the Amul brand [3].
- 1973 (August): Amul celebrates 25 years [6].
- 1976: The film Manthan, produced by GCMMF, is funded by 500,000 farmers at Rs 2 each [11].
- 1985 (end): 43,000 village co-operatives with 4.25 million milk producers are part of Operation Flood [4].
- 1998: India becomes the world's largest milk producer [4][5].

## Decision points
### D1 · 1946 · Break free of the middlemen
- Situation: Farmers in Kaira had to sell milk at whatever price contractors fixed; Polsons supplied the Bombay Milk Scheme and no one set a fair price for farmers [1]. Sardar Patel advised them to form their own co-operative with its own plant, and to stop selling if refused [1].
- Options: (a) ask the government for a fair price and keep selling through contractors; (b) form a village society in every village, demand the government buy from your Union, and strike if refused; (c) form societies but keep selling to Polson while you negotiate.
- What they did: (b). The Samarkha meeting (4 Jan 1946) set the demand; the government refused; a 15-day strike followed; the demand was accepted; the Union was registered 14 Dec 1946 [1].
- What happened next: Pasteurising began June 1948 with two societies and about 250 litres a day; by the end of 1948, 432 farmers and 5,000 litres a day [1].

### D2 · 1953 · Too much milk in winter
- Situation: Winter milk was about 2.5 times summer milk; the Bombay scheme could not take it; farmers were selling surplus cheap to middlemen again [1]; "milk holidays" stopped farmers' deliveries; experts said buffalo milk could not be spray-dried [2][7].
- Options: (a) refuse extra milk in winter (milk holidays); (b) sell the surplus to private butter makers; (c) build a factory to make butter and milk powder from buffalo milk.
- What they did: (c). A Rs 50 lakh factory, foundation 15 Nov 1954, opened 31 Oct 1955, with UNICEF, New Zealand and FAO help [1]; the first buffalo milk spray-dryer, 1955 [2].
- What happened next: Amul could accept milk all year and steady prices [2]; the brand Amul was introduced [1]; cheese and baby food followed [1][2].

### D3 · 1964–65 · Share the model with India?
- Situation: The Prime Minister visited Anand in October 1964 and wanted the Amul model copied across India [1].
- Options: (a) keep the know-how in Kaira; (b) grow Amul itself by buying milk across India and running it from Anand; (c) help farmers everywhere build their own co-operatives on the same model.
- What they did: (c). NDDB was set up at Anand in 1965 [1][10]; it launched Operation Flood, based on the "Anand Pattern" [1][4].
- What happened next: Phase I (from 1970) linked 18 milk sheds to four big cities [4]; by end of 1985, 43,000 village co-ops and 4.25 million producers [4].

### D4 · 1966 · Make people love the butter
- Situation: Amul Butter's old tagline was "Purely the Best"; the brand needed a fresher campaign [8].
- Options: (a) no ads: good butter sells itself; (b) plain ads about price and purity; (c) a cheeky campaign with a mascot that comments on the news.
- What they did: (c). Amul hired an ad agency head in 1966 [3][9]; a little girl mascot to appeal to mothers and children, and a first topical ad on horse racing [8].
- What happened next: Topical ads on news from 1967 [8]; a Guinness World Record as the longest-running ad campaign [9].

### D5 · 1973 · One brand or many?
- Situation: Other district unions in Gujarat (Mehsana, Banaskantha, Baroda, Sabarkantha, Surat) had been set up on the same model [3], and were competing against each other [3].
- Options: (a) let each union fight with its own brand; (b) share trucks and shops but keep separate brands; (c) form one marketing federation and sell everything under Amul.
- What they did: (c). GCMMF was set up in 1973 as the joint marketing body; Kaira handed over the Amul brand [3].
- What happened next: Three tiers: village society, district union, state federation [3]. In 1976 GCMMF produced Manthan, funded by 500,000 farmers [11]. Today 3.6 million producers control GCMMF [3].

## What really happened
Kaira farmers struck for 15 days in 1946 and won the right to sell milk through their own Union [1]. They built a factory in 1955 that made milk powder and butter from buffalo milk, a world first, and sold it under the name Amul [1][2]. From 1965 NDDB copied the model across India through Operation Flood [1][4]. In 1966 the cheeky Amul girl ads began [3][8]. In 1973 the Gujarat unions formed GCMMF to sell under one brand, Amul [3]. By the end of 1985, 4.25 million producers were in 43,000 village co-operatives [4], and in 1998 India became the world's largest milk producer [4].

## People (by role)
- Chairman of the Kaira Union, 1946 to early 1970s: Tribhuvandas Kishibhai Patel (died 1994) [6].
- Leader who advised the farmers: Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel (died 1950) [1].
- Organiser of the 1946 meeting and strike: Morarji Desai (died 1995) [1].
- General manager and later NDDB head: Verghese Kurien (died 2012) [5].
- Dairy engineer behind the buffalo milk spray-dryer: Harichand Megha Dalaya (died 2004) [2][7].
- Ad agency head behind the Amul girl: Sylvester daCunha (died 2023) [8][9].
- Prime Ministers who opened the 1955 factory and visited in 1964: Jawaharlal Nehru, Lal Bahadur Shastri [1].
