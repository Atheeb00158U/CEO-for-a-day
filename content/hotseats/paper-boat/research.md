## Timeline

- 2009–2011: First product, the protein drink Frissia, is dropped [1][5].
- 2011 (March): Tzinga energy drink launches at ₹25, against Red Bull at ₹95 [1]. (Wharton says 2012 [5].)
- 2011 or 2012: A co-founder brings home-made aam panna to work; a guest cannot find it in any shop; the idea is born [1][2]. Open says Sept 2011 [6]; Wharton says summer 2012 [5].
- 2013: Tzinga sells about 1.5 million packs a month [1].
- 2013-02: Test market [1][2]. Packs are placed in 500 modern stores in four metros with no ads, and sell fast [9].
- 2013-08: Paper Boat launches in Delhi and Bengaluru with two drinks (jaljeera and aam panna) [1][2][6], in 250 ml paper-look pouches [2]. Capacity about 1 million packs a month [5].
- 2013–2015: No TV or print ads; drawings of childhood memories on Facebook [7]. Packs carry short childhood stories and hidden messages [2][7]. IndiGo serves the drinks [7][10].
- 2014: A co-founder leaves; a bigger plant opens in Mysuru [3]. Revenue about ₹16 crore [7].
- 2014-02: Push to Indians living abroad begins [4].
- 2014-11: The Postcard Campaign: Paper Boat pays postage for letters to parents from 80 office canteens [8].
- 2015-01: CEO says a ₹10 crore TV campaign is planned for March [4].

- 2015: Paper Boat is about 90% of Hector's sales [1]. Tzinga is pulled after an FSSAI ruling [6]. Sales about ₹24–25 crore [6][7]. About ₹180 crore raised in July [6]. Distribution deal with Indo Nissin [1].

## Decision points

### D1 · 2011–2012 · Keep pushing the energy drink, or bottle aam panna?
- Situation: Tzinga sells cheap against Red Bull [1] and does well with students [6], but energy drinks suit only one age and one moment [1]. The founders cannot find aam panna or other old Indian drinks in any shop [1][2].
- Options: (a) put the money behind Tzinga, the drink you already sell; (b) launch fruit juices, a big market buyers know; (c) bottle traditional drinks like aam panna, an untested idea.
- What they did: (c). Test market Feb 2013, launch Aug 2013 [1][2].
- What happened next: By 2015 Paper Boat was about 90% of sales [1]; Tzinga was pulled after an FSSAI ruling [6].
- Tests: **2.1.1** (sell what people already struggle with). Supported: people wanted these drinks and could not buy them [1][2].

### D2 · 2013 · The pack: cartons, bottles or pouches?
- Situation: The drink must look homely but premium, and travel cheaply. Packs cost money on every unit sold [1][10].
- Options: (a) Tetra Pak cartons, which shops and buyers know; (b) PET bottles; (c) paper-look stand-up pouches, which are new and unproven for drinks.
- What they did: (c). 250 ml pouches that look like paper [2]. They replaced Tetra Packs with the pouch [10].
- What happened next: The pouch cost about 10% of MRP, like PET and cheaper than Tetra Pak [1]; other sources say about 50% cheaper than Tetra Pak [2][10], and lighter to ship [6][10]. Dabur later copied the pouch [5]. Cartons came only in 2016–17, for bigger family packs [3].
- Tests: **1.1.2** (know your margin on every item). Partly supported: the pack's cost share is reported [1][2][10], but full margins, MRP splits and retailer margins are not. The story can use "the pouch costs less per pack" but must not invent margin numbers.

### D3 · 2013–2014 · Getting people to try it
- Situation: Nobody has bought bottled jaljeera before. Kiranas don't push a brand people don't ask for [4]. Money is tight: the founders and early backers put in about ₹18.5 crore in all (our sum of the figures in [1]).
- Options: (a) big TV ads to make the name known fast; (b) put the drink in people's hands first: quiet placement in stores, flights and office canteens, with free tastings, and spend later; (c) cut the price to cola level to win trial.
- What they did: (b), broadly. Silent placement in 500 stores with no ads [9]; IndiGo flights and office canteens as early channels [4][7][10]; no TV or print for about two years [7]; a free postage campaign in office canteens [8]; full refunds to shops for unsold stock [10]. Free sampling drives in malls, offices and colleges are reported only by a blog [11]. Price stayed premium, about ₹30–35 [5][6].
- What happened next: Packs sold fast enough to raise capacity plans [9]; capacity grew to about 8 million packs a month and outlets to about 120,000 [5].
- Tests: **4.2.1** (give first, and people want to give back). Partly supported. "Give first" is clear in the free postcards [8] and the shop refund policy [10]; free tastings need a primary source (see Open questions). Write the real option as "let people try it before you advertise" rather than "free tastings" unless the afaqs! June 2015 piece is found.

### D4 · 2014–2015 · What the brand says: health or memories?
- Situation: The drinks have no preservatives, colours or fizz [2]. Rivals sell fruit juice and cola [4]. Analysts doubt nostalgia works with the young [1][5].
- Options: (a) lead with health and ingredients ("no preservatives, real fruit"); (b) lead with childhood memories, and let the product speak for health; (c) lead with price and value against colas.
- What they did: (b). The name and tagline "Drinks and Memories" call up childhood [2][3]. Ads and posts were about childhood memories [1][7]. The brand mixed "authentic" with "alive" so it would not look old [5].
- What happened next: TV ads in 2015 by Gulzar [1][3]; sales doubled in a year [1]; about ₹180 crore raised [6]. One commentator said it conveyed "natural, no preservatives" without saying it [7].
- Tests: **4.3.2** (sell what you stand for, not just specs). Supported.

### D5 · 2013–2015 · The voice on the pack
- Situation: The pouch is the brand's main way to talk to buyers before any ads [7]. Early on, many buyers first meet it on a flight or a shop shelf [4][7].
- Options: (a) plain labels: name, flavour, ingredients; (b) a short, warm story or joke on every pack; (c) a famous film star on the pack.
- What they did: (b). Short childhood stories in the pack graphics [2]; playful lines on packs [6]; stories like "climbing mango trees" and hidden messages at the bottom [7].
- What happened next: Early on, packs on IndiGo were the brand's biggest ad [7]. "Innocence" became the base of all its communication [7].
- Tests: **4.3.3** (give your brand a voice people enjoy). Supported. Option (c) is our estimate and needs care: say "a film star", never a real name.

## What really happened
Hector Beverages sold a cheap energy drink, Tzinga [1]. The founders saw that old Indian drinks like aam panna were not sold in shops [1][2]. In August 2013 they launched Paper Boat in paper-look pouches that cost less than cartons [1][2][10]. They let people find it in stores, flights and offices before spending on TV [4][7][9]. The packs told small childhood stories [2][7], and the brand said "Drinks and Memories" [3]. By 2015 Paper Boat was about 90% of sales, sales about ₹25 crore a year, and investors put in about ₹180 crore [1][6][7]. Likely `realEnding`: survivor or legend (Atheeb's call; it grew fast but profit was not disclosed [6]).
