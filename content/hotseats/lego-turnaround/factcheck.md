## Other issues

- Living people: none named. Only companies are named (Merlin Entertainments, Blackstone, Flextronics, Mattel).
- Anachronisms: none found (Harry Potter sets from 2001, LEGOLAND Deutschland 2002, so "four parks" in 2004 is right).
- Overstated wording: "kept 30% of Merlin" (should be "took a 30% stake"); "about $500 million" box office (final $470.8m); "biggest toy company ... by sales" in 2015 (half-year only); "biggest loss ever" for 2003 (2004 was bigger); "to 6,000" pieces rests on one opened source (another gives 6,500); "the money helped LEGO fix its main business" is unsourced.
- Path logic: nodes.d4.options.cut.outcome real variant is reached in 2005, but [4] and [16] give no date for the piece cut, and [16] says it took several years. Consider "LEGO began cutting..." to avoid implying the whole cut was finished in 2005.

## Summary
Claims checked: 61. Supported: 55 (2 with wording fixes). Unsupported: 1. Contradicted: 4 (one of them depends on another).

Most important fixes:
1. "By 2015 LEGO was the biggest toy company in the world by sales" (legend real variant and realHistory) is contradicted: Mattel had higher full-year 2015 revenue. Only half-year (H1 2014 / H1 2015) or profit leadership is supported. Also reword "was now the leader".
2. realHistory: "its biggest loss ever" for 2003 is wrong, because the 2004 net loss (kr 1.93bn) was bigger.
3. The LEGO Movie gross: change "about $500 million" to about $470 million.
4. "kept 30% of Merlin" should be "took a 30% stake in Merlin". Remove or soften the unsourced "The money helped LEGO fix its main business".
5. Pieces: use "about 13,000 to about 6,000–6,500" or "about half", and don't tie the completed cut to 2005.

Conflicting figures resolved: 2003 loss, use DKK 1.4bn pre-tax ([5], [1]); BrickNerd's 1.1bn is likely the net figure; Wharton's $300m matches neither and should not be used. Park sale, use €375m for four parks plus a 30% Merlin stake ([1], [6]); Wharton's "$460m for 70%" is the outlier. The Flextronics outsourcing from 2006 and its 2008 reversal are fully supported by LEGO's own history page [7].
