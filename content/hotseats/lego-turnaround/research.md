## Timeline
- 1968: The first LEGOLAND park opens in Billund, Denmark [8].
- 1993: Sales slow; LEGO adds toys but does not sell more items overall [2].
- 1996: LEGOLAND Windsor opens in the UK [8]. LEGO creates Lego Media to make software, games and videos [1][3].
- 1998: LEGO posts the first loss in its history [3].
- 1999: LEGO launches Star Wars sets [4]; thanks largely to them, it is profitable again [3]. LEGOLAND California opens [8].
- 2000: LEGO loses kr 831 million, cuts 1,500 jobs and drops books, clothing, watches and a planned fifth park [4].
- 2001: Bionicle launches and is a hit [4]. 500 more jobs are cut [4].
- 2002: Explore replaces DUPLO for US customers [4]; parents do not see it as LEGO and it costs about DKK 500m [6]. Galidor launches with a TV show [10]. LEGOLAND Deutschland opens [8].
- 2003: Pre-tax loss of about DKK 1.4 billion, the biggest in LEGO's history [6][1]; the company is "virtually out of cash" [2].
- 2004: About 1,000 jobs cut [1]; debts near $1 billion [5]; the owner puts kr 800 million of private money in and steps down as president; a new CEO takes over [1][6]. Lego Interactive (games) is closed [1][11]. DUPLO returns [4]. Net loss for the year: kr 1.93 billion [7].
- 2005: Four LEGOLAND parks sold to Merlin Entertainments (Blackstone) for €375 million; LEGO keeps 30% of Merlin [1][7]. LEGO Star Wars video game, made by partners, launches in March [11]. Pieces cut from about 13,000 to 6,000 [5]. Net profit kr 505 million, revenue up 12% to kr 7.05 billion [7].
- 2006: Shared Vision plan moves much production, especially DUPLO, to Flextronics [9].
- 2008: After shortages, LEGO ends the Flextronics deal and takes production back [9].
- 2009: LEGO opens its own factories in Nyíregyháza (Hungary) and Monterrey (Mexico) [9].
- 2014: The LEGO Movie earns about $500 million; H1 revenue is more than three times H1 2008; LEGO passes Mattel in half-year sales [12][1].
- 2015-H1: LEGO is the world's largest toy company by revenue [1].

## Decision points
### D1 · 1999 · How far beyond the brick?
- Situation: Sales stalled from 1993 and more products did not mean more sales [2]. Video game consoles were changing play [3]. LEGO posted its first loss in 1998 [3].
- Options: (a) go big: licences, parks, games, clothes, many new products; (b) take the Star Wars licence but stay with brick-based sets only; (c) refuse licences and screens, stick to classic sets.
- What the company did: Star Wars sets in 1999 [4], LEGOLAND California 1999 and Germany 2002 [8], electronics, games, jewellery, education centres; it tripled product offerings [2].
- What happened next: Profit in 1999 thanks to Star Wars [3], then a kr 831 million loss in 2000, 1,500 job cuts, and clothing, watches and books dropped [4].

### D2 · 2002 · New toys for a new age
- Situation: Licensed sets were hits only in film years [2]. Bionicle, a brick-built story line, was a 2001 hit [4].
- Options: (a) replace DUPLO with Explore in the US and launch Galidor; (b) more Bionicle-style, brick-built lines; (c) discount existing sets.
- What the company did: Explore replaced DUPLO for US customers [4]; Galidor launched with a company-funded TV show [6][10].
- What happened next: Explore did not sell and cost about DKK 500m [6]; Galidor sold poorly [10]; DUPLO came back in 2004 [4].

### D3 · 2004 · Stop the bleeding
- Situation: 2003 pre-tax loss of DKK 1.4 billion [6]; nearly $1 billion of debt [5]; some kits cost more to make than they sold for [6]; new CEO [1].
- Options: (a) borrow more and keep everything; (b) sell the parks, close the games unit, bring back DUPLO, focus on the brick; (c) cut costs everywhere but keep all businesses.
- What the company did: ~1,000 jobs cut and games unit closed in 2004 [1]; DUPLO returned [4]; four parks sold for €375 million in 2005 with a 30% Merlin stake [1][7][8].
- What happened next: Former games staff founded a publisher; LEGO Star Wars (2005) sold 3.3 million by March 2006 [11].

### D4 · 2005 · Too many pieces
- Situation: LEGO made about 13,000 different pieces [5].
- Options: (a) cut pieces by about half; (b) keep all pieces for designers' freedom; (c) fewer sets, same pieces.
- What the company did: Cut to about 6,000 pieces and dropped unpopular products [5]; put profit before growth [7].
- What happened next: 2005 net profit kr 505 million after a kr 1.93 billion loss in 2004; revenue +12% to kr 7.05 billion, led by Bionicle, DUPLO, LEGO City and LEGO Star Wars [7].

### D5 · 2006 · Who makes the bricks?
- Situation: LEGO was closing a Swiss plant and planned to move production to low-wage countries [7].
- Options: (a) keep old factories; (b) outsource to Flextronics; (c) own factories in cheaper countries.
- What the company did: Outsourced much production, especially DUPLO, to Flextronics from 2006 [9].
- What happened next: Shortages; Flextronics could not make enough DUPLO flowers; deal ended 2008; own plants opened in Hungary and Mexico in 2009 [9].

## What really happened
LEGO's push beyond the brick in the late 1990s and early 2000s, into parks, games, clothes, electronics and non-brick toys, ended in record losses in 2003 and 2004 [6][7]. A new CEO cut jobs, closed the games unit, sold the parks and halved the number of pieces [1][5][7]. LEGO was profitable again in 2005 [7], kept growing, and by 2015 was the world's largest toy company by revenue [1][12].
