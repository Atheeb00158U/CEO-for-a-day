## Timeline
- 2014-03: UP food safety officials in Barabanki picked up a Maggi batch for a routine check, and the Gorakhpur lab reported lead at 17.2 ppm against a 2 ppm limit, plus MSG that the label did not declare [10].
- 2014-04: Nestlé appealed, and the central referral lab in Kolkata confirmed the lead and MSG findings [10].
- 2015-04: UP authorities ordered a recall of a batch made in February 2014; Nestlé said that batch had passed its best-before date in November 2014 [10][8].
- 2015-06-01/02: Nestlé said more than 1,000 batches had been tested and lead was within limits [8]. Delhi reported 10 of 13 samples above the 2.5 ppm limit and banned sales for 15 days [8].
- 2015-06-03: Nestlé India's shares fell about 10%, the biggest one-day drop in nine years [8].
- 2015-06-05: Nestlé announced it was withdrawing Maggi from shelves across India [9]. The same day FSSAI ordered a recall of the nine approved variants and called them unsafe [1][9]. At least six states, several big retailers and the Indian army had already banned Maggi [9].
- 2015-06 to 2015-09: About 38,000 tonnes of noodles were destroyed by 1 September 2015, burned in cement plants [17].
- 2015-07: Nestlé India reported a net loss of Rs 64.4 crore for the April–June quarter, after Rs 451.66 crore of exceptional items [12].
- 2015-07: Nestlé moved its India managing director to Switzerland and named an Indian executive from its Philippines unit to take over on 1 August [11].
- 2015-08-11: The Department of Consumer Affairs filed a complaint against Nestlé at the NCDRC [1].
- 2015-08-13: The Bombay High Court set aside the ban and ordered retests in three NABL-accredited labs [7].
- 2015-10: All 90 samples (six variants) cleared the court-ordered tests, and production restarted at three plants [13].
- 2015-11-09: Maggi went back on sale in 100 cities, with an online sales partner [15].
- 2015-12: FSSAI's appeal against the Bombay HC orders reached the Supreme Court, which issued notice to Nestlé [6].
- 2016-10: Nestlé put Maggi's share of the instant-noodles market at 57%, against 75% before the crisis [16].
- 2019-01-03: The Supreme Court sent the consumer case back to the NCDRC to decide on the basis of the CFTRI lab report [5].
- 2024-04-02: The NCDRC dismissed the government's complaint, which had sought about Rs 640 crore [1][2].

## Decision points
### D1 · 2014 · The first bad lab report
- Situation: A state lab said one batch had lead far above the limit and MSG despite a "No added MSG" label [10].
- Options a CEO realistically had: (a) contest the result through the official appeal route; (b) fix the label and order fresh independent tests, then tell the public; (c) quietly pull the batch and say nothing.
- What the company did: Nestlé used the appeal route and asked for the samples to go to the Kolkata referral lab [10].
- What happened next: The referral lab confirmed the findings, and the issue grew into a recall order and a prosecution decision in 2015 [10].

### D2 · May–June 2015 · Defend or retreat?
- Situation: States began testing Maggi, Delhi reported failed samples, and the share price fell sharply [8].
- Options: (a) defend the product with your own test data; (b) voluntarily withdraw stock in the worst-hit states; (c) halt sales nationwide at once.
- What the company did: Nestlé said its own and external tests showed lead within limits, and that only UP had ordered a recall [8].
- What happened next: More states banned sales and big retailers dropped the product [9]. Analysts widely criticised the response as slow [11].

### D3 · 5 June 2015 · Pull every packet?
- Situation: The bans were spreading, and Maggi made up about 30% of Nestlé India's sales [9][14].
- Options: (a) keep selling where it was still legal; (b) withdraw from all of India while saying the product is safe; (c) withdraw and admit fault.
- What the company did: Nestlé announced a nationwide withdrawal, and its global CEO held a press conference in Delhi [9].
- What happened next: FSSAI ordered a recall the same day [1][9]. Nestlé destroyed about 38,000 tonnes of stock [17] and reported its first quarterly loss in at least 15 years [12][14].

### D4 · June–Aug 2015 · Fight the regulator in court?
- Situation: The FSSAI order called the noodles unsafe and covered all nine variants [7].
- Options: (a) accept the order and retest quietly; (b) challenge it in the High Court; (c) negotiate privately with the regulator.
- What the company did: Nestlé challenged the order in the Bombay High Court [1][7].
- What happened next: The court found that natural justice was not followed, that the labs were not accredited, and that the ban covered variants that were never tested; it ordered retests [7]. FSSAI then appealed to the Supreme Court [6].

### D5 · July 2015 · Change the leader?
- Situation: The company was fighting the regulator in court while its handling of the crisis was being criticised [11].
- Options: (a) keep the existing team; (b) bring in an Indian leader with group experience; (c) hire an outside crisis manager.
- What the company did: Nestlé moved its India MD to Switzerland and appointed an Indian executive who had run Nestlé Philippines [11].
- What happened next: The new leader took charge from 1 August 2015 [11], and the relaunch came three months later [15].

### D6 · Oct–Nov 2015 · How to come back
- Situation: All the court-ordered tests came back clear [13].
- Options: (a) relaunch the full range at once; (b) relaunch the core Masala variant first in a big festive push; (c) launch under a new brand.
- What the company did: Nestlé waited until new batches also cleared the tests, then relaunched in 100 cities at Diwali with an online partner [13][15].
- What happened next: By October 2016 Maggi's share was back to 57%, against rivals including ITC's Yippee and Patanjali [16].

## What really happened
A routine lab test in one UP district in 2014 grew into a nationwide ban in June 2015 [10][9]. Nestlé first defended the product and then pulled it everywhere, and it destroyed about 38,000 tonnes of noodles [8][9][17]. It won in the Bombay High Court on process grounds and passed the retests, and Maggi returned in November 2015 [7][13][15]. Market share recovered to 57% within a year but stayed below its pre-crisis level [16]. The government's consumer case was dismissed in 2024 after a central lab found lead within limits [1][3].

## Risks
- **Litigation status (key):**
  - The government's NCDRC consumer case was dismissed on 2 April 2024 [1][2]. I found no report of an appeal against that dismissal, but I could not confirm either way.
  - FSSAI's Supreme Court appeal against the 2015 Bombay HC orders, SLP (C) 33251/2015, was filed and admitted for notice [6]. The 2019 Supreme Court order said that appeal was separate [5]. I found no source showing it was ever decided, so it may still be pending.
  - Criminal prosecutions from 2015 under the Food Safety Act were filed in several states. Nagpur's case was quashed in January 2025 [18], and Uttarakhand's batch was quashed in July 2026 [19]. A Barabanki court summoned a Nestlé executive in 2015 [10], and I found no outcome for that case.
