## Timeline
- 2004: India opens commercial aviation to competition; about six new airlines launch [7].
- 2005: India's economy grows 6.9% in the year to March; the government expects air travel to grow up to 20% a year; Airbus forecasts 100 million new potential flyers by 2010 [2].
- 2005-06-16: At the Paris Air Show, new low-cost airline IndiGo, backed by travel group InterGlobe, orders 100 Airbus A320-family jets worth $6 billion at list prices [1]. Wikipedia calls it a firm order [3].
- 2005-06: Kingfisher orders A380 superjumbos; Jet Airways splits an order between Airbus and Boeing [2].
- 2006-07-28: IndiGo takes its first A320 [3].
- 2006-08-04: First IndiGo flight, Delhi to Imphal via Guwahati [3].
- 2006: IndiGo launches with one class, no business seats, no loyalty programme and no free meals, and promises on-time flights [4]. Jet, Kingfisher and Air India together hold nearly 80% of the market [4].
- 2006: Six planes by year end; nine more added in 2007 [3].
- 2007: Fuel prices rise, fares go up and India's airline boom begins to fade [7].
- 2007-05-31: Kingfisher's parent UB Holdings buys 26% of Air Deccan, India's first low-cost airline [6].
- 2008: Indian airlines lose $2.5 billion, a quarter of world airline losses [7]. Jet Airways and Kingfisher form an operating alliance [9].
- FY2008/09: IndiGo makes a USD17.6M profit; Kingfisher loses USD342.7M, Jet USD86.1M, SpiceJet USD75.4M [8].
- 2009: IndiGo takes its 25th plane [3].
- 2009-11: IndiGo's on-time rate is 78.4% against an industry 74.5% [5].
- 2010-12: IndiGo is India's third-largest airline with 17.3% of passengers, ahead of Air India [3].
- 2011-01-12: IndiGo agrees to buy 180 more A320-family jets (150 A320neo, 30 A320), $15.6B at list prices, with A320neo deliveries from 2016 [11].
- 2011-09-01: First international flight, Delhi to Dubai [3].
- 2011-10: IndiGo has 19.6% share and is the only profitable major Indian airline; Kingfisher cancels many flights [12].
- 2012-03: IndiGo moves up to second place by share [13].
- 2012-10-01: Kingfisher's fleet is grounded by a strike over unpaid wages; its licence is suspended later that month; debt estimated at US$2.49B [15]. IndiGo, SpiceJet and Jet add flights [15].
- 2012: IndiGo carries more passengers than any other Indian airline [14]; 50 million passengers flown by December [3].
- FY2012-13: IndiGo net profit Rs 7.87 billion, six times the year before [10].

## Decision points
### D1 · 2005 · How big to start?
- Situation: India had just opened its skies [7]; growth forecasts were huge [2]. Kingfisher was ordering superjumbos [2]. Jet, Kingfisher and Air India held nearly 80% [4].
- Options: (a) lease a few mixed second-hand planes and grow slowly; (b) order 100 new planes of one type at once; (c) buy a mix of jets of different sizes to fly every kind of route.
- What the company did: ordered 100 A320-family jets at the Paris Air Show, $6B at list prices, before its first flight [1][3].
- What happened next: first plane 28 July 2006, first flight 4 August 2006 [3].

### D2 · 2006 · What kind of airline?
- Situation: Kingfisher was the glamour airline [2]; Air Deccan sold very low fares but was struggling [4][7]. A 15–20 minute late takeoff was normal [4].
- Options: (a) full service like Kingfisher: meals, lounges, business class; (b) the lowest fares in India, even below cost (as seen in the market [7]); (c) low fares, one class, no free meals, and a promise to be on time.
- What the company did: (c). One class, no business seats, no loyalty programme, no free meals; snacks for sale; on-time promise at the centre of its marketing; A320-only so crews worked faster; 20–30 minute turnarounds [4].
- What happened next: In Nov 2009 IndiGo's on-time rate was 78.4% against 74.5% for the industry (Kingfisher was 82.5%) [5].

### D3 · 2006–2010 · How to pay for 100 planes?
- Situation: 100 planes at $6B list [1]; a young airline.
- Options: (a) borrow from banks and own every plane; (b) sell each new plane to a leasing firm and rent it back, and swap it for a new one after about six years; (c) save money with cheaper old planes.
- What the company did: (b). It bought in bulk at a discount, sold planes to lessors around delivery, leased them back and retired them within six years, avoiding depreciation and heavy maintenance and keeping its balance sheet light [10]. Young planes meant fewer maintenance delays [4].
- What happened next: covered in D4/D5 results; method was still in use in 2014 [10].

### D4 · 2008 · The year the industry lost its way
- Situation: Fuel prices rose and the boom faded from 2007 [7]. Kingfisher's parent bought 26% of Air Deccan [6]; Jet and Kingfisher formed an alliance [9]. Indian airlines lost $2.5B in 2008 [7].
- Options: (a) buy a struggling rival to grow fast; (b) stay on your own, keep one plane type and tight costs, keep adding planes; (c) park half the fleet and wait.
- What the company did: kept flying only A320s with its low-cost model [4], kept adding planes (25th in 2009) [3]. (No source says IndiGo considered a merger; the options are realistic, not reported.)
- What happened next: FY2008/09 profit USD17.6M while Kingfisher lost USD342.7M, Jet USD86.1M, SpiceJet USD75.4M [8].

### D5 · 2011 · The next big order
- Situation: IndiGo third-largest with 17.3% in Dec 2010 [3]. Airbus was launching the A320neo [11].
- Options: (a) pause orders and build cash; (b) add big long-range jets for international routes; (c) order 180 more A320-family planes including the new A320neo.
- What the company did: (c), 12 Jan 2011, 180 planes, $15.6B list [11]; first international route Delhi–Dubai on 1 Sept 2011, flown with its A320s [3].
- What happened next: Oct 2011 share 19.6%, only profitable major airline [12]; second place by March 2012 [13]; first by passengers in 2012 [14].

## What really happened
IndiGo began with one bold order for 100 A320s in 2005 [1] and flew its first flight in August 2006 [3]. It ran one plane type, one class, no free meals and an on-time promise [4], and paid for planes with sale and leaseback [10]. While Indian airlines lost $2.5 billion in 2008 [7], IndiGo made a profit in FY2008/09 [8]. In 2011 it ordered 180 more planes [11] and began flying abroad [3]. In October 2012 Kingfisher, the luxury airline, was grounded and lost its licence [15]. In 2012 IndiGo carried more passengers than any other Indian airline [14], and in FY2012-13 its profit rose sixfold to Rs 7.87 billion [10].

## Risks
- **Co-founder dispute (living people, legal):** In July 2019 one co-founder wrote to SEBI alleging governance lapses and related-party deals; the other's group filed for LCIA arbitration on 1 Oct 2019 [18][19]. InterGlobe Aviation settled the SEBI matter for Rs 2.10 crore in Feb 2021 [18]. The final LCIA award came on 23 Sept 2021 [18]; one side said it would challenge it, and a Delhi High Court enforcement step was reported [19]. Share-transfer limits were dropped at a Dec 2021 EGM and the co-founder left the board in Feb 2022, then sold down in stages to 4.71% by Aug 2025 [19][20]. I could not confirm whether any challenge to the award is still pending. Kept fully out of the story; both co-founders are described only as "a co-founder" and are not named in hotseat.json.
- **Live regulatory/legal matters today (not central to 2005–2012):** CCI ordered a competition investigation on 4 Feb 2026 after IndiGo's mass cancellations in Dec 2025; DGCA cut its schedule and imposed penalties [21]. Other Delhi High Court tax and customs cases were seen in search results but not opened. None of this is in the story, but IndiGo is a company with live litigation and a live CCI probe; Atheeb should decide whether that is acceptable.
- **Kingfisher's collapse and its chairman:** Kingfisher's debts and grounding [15] are part of the story's backdrop; its chairman faces well-known legal cases in India and the UK (not researched here). The story names only the airline, never the person, and makes no claim about him.
- **Living people central:** both co-founders and the former president are living; described by role only.
- **Thin/conflicting sources:** Business Standard, Mint and Reuters' own site blocked the fetcher. The 2005 order is called a "letter of intent" by some 2005 reports but "firm" by Wikipedia; the story says only "ordered". Wikipedia gives both "17 August 2012" and "December 2012" for becoming largest; the story says "in 2012" per Skift [14]. The HBS student posts [4][17] are secondary; [17] has clear errors and is not used on the real path. Nov 2009 on-time data shows Kingfisher ahead of IndiGo [5], so the story does not claim IndiGo was the most punctual then.
- **Sale and leaseback:** described in [10]; exact gains per plane come only from unnamed sources and are not used.
