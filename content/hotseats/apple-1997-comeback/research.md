# Apple's 1997 comeback: research brief
Hot Seat id: apple-1997-comeback · Years covered: 1996–1999 · Researched: 2026-10-27

## Sources

4. [Microsoft and Apple Affirm Commitment to Build Next Generation Software for Macintosh (Microsoft press release, 6 Aug 1997)](https://news.microsoft.com/1997/08/06/microsoft-and-apple-affirm-commitment-to-build-next-generation-software-for-macintosh/): $150M in non-voting Apple stock; broad patent cross-licence; future Office versions for Mac; Internet Explorer as the Mac OS default browser; Java compatibility.

## Decision points
### D1 · 1996 · Where to get a modern operating system
- Situation: Apple lost $816M in FY1996 [9]. Its promised new OS, Copland, was badly late [2].
- Options a CEO realistically had (3, incl. what the company did): (a) give Copland about 18 more months [2]; (b) buy Be, whose BeOS was in beta, for about the $300M Be asked [2]; (c) buy NeXT and use NEXTSTEP [1].
- What the company did: Apple agreed to buy NeXT for $400 million on 20 Dec 1996; the co-founder came back as an adviser [1].
- What happened next: FY1997 results carried write-offs for the NeXT purchase [9]; the co-founder's role grew after the CEO left in July 1997 [3].

### D2 · Aug 1997 · Make peace with Microsoft?
- Situation: Apple was losing money [5][9]; it and Microsoft had fought a user-interface lawsuit for years [5]; Microsoft had made Mac software since Excel in 1985 [4].
- Options: (a) keep fighting; (b) raise money another way (debt or new shares); (c) take Microsoft's investment and settle.
- What the company did: took $150M in non-voting stock, a patent cross-licence, a promise of future Office for Mac, and made Internet Explorer the default browser [4].
- What happened next: part of the Macworld crowd booed; Apple stock rose 35% to $26.75 [5].

### D3 · 1997 · Too many products and the clones
- Situation: dozens of Macs plus printers, cameras and more, few profitable [7]; clone makers paid $80 a machine [7]; Mac OS computer sales had fallen almost 20% in two years [6].
- Options: (a) keep everything and grow clones; (b) trim a few products, keep clones; (c) cut to a four-box grid and end clones.
- What the company did: cut more than 70% of products to the grid [7], stopped clone licences and bought Power Computing's core assets for $10M cash + $100M stock [6], ended Newton in Feb 1998 [11].
- What happened next: 3,000+ jobs cut [7]; quarterly operating costs fell from $505M to $353M [10]; Q1 FY1998 was profitable ($47M) [15].

### D4 · Sept 1997 · How to talk about Apple
- Situation: losses and a damaged name; the 1984 ad agency was available again [8].
- Options: (a) cut ads to save cash; (b) ads comparing speed and price with PCs; (c) a brand campaign about Apple's values.
- What the company did: "Think different" with TBWA Chiat/Day, two 60-second TV ads from 28 Sept 1997 [8].
- What happened next: FY1997 still ended with a $1.0B loss [9], but the next quarter was profitable [15].

### D5 · 1998 · A computer for ordinary buyers
- Situation: the consumer-desktop box in the grid needed a product [7].
- Options: (a) a cheap beige Mac like a PC; (b) skip homes and serve loyal pro and school users; (c) a new all-in-one, internet-first Mac.
- What the company did: the iMac, $1,299, translucent case, USB, no floppy, shown 6 May 1998, in shops 15 Aug 1998 [12][13].
- What happened next: 278,000 sold in six weeks; 29.4% of early buyers were first-time buyers [14]; 519,000 sold in the Dec 1998 quarter [15].

## What really happened
Apple lost $816M in FY1996 and $1.0B in FY1997 [9]. It bought NeXT [1], took $150M from Microsoft and settled their fight [4][5], cut more than 70% of its products [7], ended the clones [6], launched "Think different" [8] and the iMac [12]. It made a profit from the quarter to Dec 1997 [15], earned $309M in FY1998, its first profitable year since 1995 [14], and made $152M in the Dec 1998 quarter [15].

## Risks
- Litigation today: none found. The 1990s Apple–Microsoft lawsuit was settled in 1997 [5].
- Living people: the 1996–97 CEO, Microsoft's co-founder and Be's founder are living. They appear only by role and no blame or motives are stated. The returning co-founder died in 2011; still no quotes or private thoughts are used.
- Thin/conflicting sources: Apple's own 1990s site and big newspapers were not fetched; much comes from Apple press releases reprinted by MacTech plus TidBITS/Macworld.
