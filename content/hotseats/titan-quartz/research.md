## Timeline
- 1961: HMT starts making wristwatches in Bangalore with Citizen of Japan [14].
- 1970s: HMT makes some quartz watches, but they sell poorly because they cost more; it stays with mechanical watches [14].
- 1975–76: HMT's watch units turn over more than Rs 13 crore; demand is put at 5 million watches a year [19].
- 1977 (21 March): A Tata colleague phones a Tata Press manager and picks watches as the best of five project ideas [4]. (Business Today 2002 says 1976 [17].)
- 1979: TIDCO, which holds a watch licence, invites Tata to take up the project [3][10].
- Late 1970s–mid 1980s: The sector is reserved for public and small-scale firms, so the Tatas are first refused; Swiss makers won't share know-how [3][15].
- 1983: Tata, TIDCO and France Ebauches form an alliance in Paris; Tamil Nadu gives 15 acres at SIPCOT Hosur [10].
- 1984 (26 July): Titan Watches Limited is incorporated in Chennai, a Tata–TIDCO joint venture [6][11]. (Some sources say 1986 [16][17].)
- About 1984–86: Titan starts in a small room at the Taj West End hotel, Bangalore [1]. It hires former HMT staff [12][14].
- 1985: A rule change lets private firms choose their own mix of mechanical and quartz watches within approved quantities [1].
- 1985–86: Titan decides to make quartz watches only [1].
- 1986: Watchmaking starts at Hosur [10]. A first-batch watch (Dec 1986) given to JRD Tata does not work [12].
- 1986 (Nov): Titan signs an MoU with Casio for 2 million digital and analog-digital watches [6]. (What came of it is not known.)
- 1987 (Feb): The first Titan watch is released [11]. First sales in 1987 [10].
- 1987: The first range: five collections, priced Rs 350–900 [2][7][8] (Penguin says Rs 350–700 [12]). Half-page newspaper ads show close-ups and prices, no celebrities [8][9].
- 1987 (June): Titan's public share issue [1].
- 1987 (23 Dec): First Titan showroom opens at Safina Plaza, Bengaluru; 17 watches sold on day one, 313 in the first month [7][12].
- 1987–88: Titan becomes India's leading maker of quartz analogue watches in its first year [5].
- 1988 (11 March): JRD Tata dedicates the Hosur factory complex [3][10].
- 1989 (10 April): The one millionth Titan watch [7]; one million sold within two years [11].
- 1990: A showroom opens at Colaba, Mumbai, opened by JRD Tata, called India's first exclusive watch-brand showroom [8][9].
- 1991: Titan starts exports [15]. HMT still makes nearly 7 million watches, more than its Indian rivals together [14].
- Early 1990s: HMT moves about a sixth of output to quartz, too late to catch Titan [14].
- 1992: Timex JV; Timex sold in Titan stores [6].
- 1993: Titan enters Europe [8]; later called a poor move [12][13].
- 1994: Tanishq (jewellery) starts [4][6].
- 2012: Titan holds over 60% of India's organised watch market [1].
- 2016 (May): HMT's watch business is shut [14].

## Decision points

### D1 · 1985 · Quartz only, or mechanical too?
- Situation: India's watch market is mechanical and HMT makes about 1 million of the 1.5 million watches made each year [8]. HMT tried quartz in the 1970s but it sold poorly because it cost more [14]. A 1985 rule change lets Titan choose its own mix [1]. Former HMT staff advise quartz [14].
- Options: (a) mechanical first, like the market leader, adding quartz later; (b) a mix of both, to spread the risk; (c) quartz only, betting on where the technology is going.
- What they did: (c). Titan chose quartz only [1][8].
- What happened next: In its first year Titan was India's leading quartz analogue maker [5]; one million watches by April 1989 [7]. HMT only moved part of its output to quartz in the early 1990s and lost share [14].
- Tests: **12.1.1** (judge new things by how fast they improve). Also supports **12.1.2** (the quartz watch that looked costly and unpopular in the 1970s [14]); the curriculum keeps 12.1.2 quiz-only. Told from the attacker's seat: HMT is the incumbent that waited.

### D2 · 1986–87 · How to reach buyers
- Situation: Watches are sold through existing shops; buyers wait months or apply to HMT [2][15]. Titan has a new brand and no shops of its own.
- Options: (a) sell only through the old watch dealers; (b) open your own showrooms only; (c) sell straight to shop owners you pick and train, and open your own showrooms too.
- What they did: Closest to (c), but thinly sourced. Titan sold directly to retail businessmen, not through set distribution channels, and trained salesmen [8]; it opened its own showroom in Bengaluru in December 1987 [7][11][12] and began an exclusive showroom chain early on [5]. By 2012 it also sold through 11,000+ multi-brand outlets [1].
- What happened next: Big crowds at the first showroom with catalogue cut-outs [7]; a famous Colaba showroom in 1990 [8].
- Tests: curriculum says **12.1.3** (+5.2.1). **Honest note:** the fit is weak. 12.1.3 is about an incumbent following new habits even when old income suffers; Titan had no old income. The real history supports "build a new channel because the old one doesn't fit a new product". **5.2.1** (master one channel first) is not clearly supported: Titan seems to have used dealers and showrooms at the same time. No source opened says the old watch dealers refused Titan or that Titan weighed "dealers vs showrooms" as one choice. Treat this call as the weakest; consider rewording it, or swapping it for D5.

### D3 · 1987 · Price for the first watches
- Situation: The first range is ready: five collections from steel to gold-plated [12]. HMT watches are seen as plain tools [11]; the rich buy smuggled imports [11][8].
- Options: (a) price low, near HMT, to win volume fast; (b) price high, close to imports, for status; (c) price as a premium Indian brand that the middle class can still afford.
- What they did: (c). The first range sold at Rs 350–900 [2][7][8] (Rs 350–700 in [12]); positioned premium from the start [12]; the aim was well-designed watches for every Indian [2].
- What happened next: 313 watches in the first month from the first showroom [12]; the gold-plated Classique was 65% of sales [12]; one million watches within two years [11].
- Tests: **3.1.1** (price from the buyer's value). Supported in spirit (premium but affordable, gold-plated best-seller); no source says how Titan worked out the price. HMT's prices in 1987 were not found, so any comparison in the story must be an estimate.

### D4 · 1987 · What the brand says
- Situation: HMT stands for durable mechanical watches [13]; buyers saw watches as plain tools [11]. Titan's watches are new, quartz and styled [7].
- Options: (a) "lasts a lifetime", match HMT on toughness; (b) low price and value; (c) style and design: a watch as something to show and gift.
- What they did: (c). Desai checked the look of every watch [4]; ads were close-ups of the watches with prices, no celebrities [8][9]; TV ads used a Mozart tune [4][11]; the brand came to stand for aspiration and modern life [15].
- What happened next: Titan ranked first among most admired durables companies in a 1999 survey [13]; became India's best-known watch company [17].
- Tests: **4.3.2** (sell what you stand for, not just specs).

### D5 (optional) · 1993 · Go to Europe?
- Situation: Titan leads at home; it wants a global name [8].
- Options: (a) enter Europe against the Swiss; (b) grow in the Middle East and Asia where many Indians live; (c) stay home and add new lines.
- What they did: (a), in 1993 [8]; it struggled with Europe's delivery and quality standards [12] and later scaled back to India, the Middle East and the Far East [13].
- Tests: no curriculum code assigned. Only if a fifth call is needed; it falls outside 1984–90.

## What really happened
Titan was set up in 1984 by Tata and TIDCO, after years of fighting rules that reserved watches for the state and small firms [3][6]. When a 1985 rule let it choose, it made only quartz watches, while HMT stayed mostly mechanical [1][14]. Its first five collections came out in 1987 at about Rs 350–900, with ads that showed the watches and their prices [2][7][8]. It opened its own showroom in Bengaluru in December 1987 [7] and sold one million watches by April 1989 [7]. HMT turned to quartz only in the early 1990s and kept losing share [14]. By 2012 Titan held over 60% of India's organised watch market [1], and HMT's watch unit shut in 2016 [14]. Ending tier: legend.

## Risks
- Litigation: none found for 1984–1990. HMT's watch unit is closed [14].
- Living people: Bhaskar Bhat (not in the story years). Status of Anil Manchanda and B.G. Dwarkanath not checked. Desai, the main figure, died in 2016 [16].
- A 2026 TV series dramatises this story [18]. Players may know its version; our text must follow our sources, not the show. The show is based on Vinay Kamath's book; we have read only the Penguin blog about it [12].
- Thin sources: most early detail comes from Tata's own pages [2][3][4][5] and admiring features [7][8][9][11]. No primary source on the dealer-vs-showroom choice or on how prices were set.
- Conflicting dates: founding 1984 [1][3][6][11] vs 1986 [16][17][18] (we use 1984 incorporation; production 1986). First collections 1987 [1][2][11] vs 1989 [9] (we use 1987). First showroom: Safina Plaza, Bengaluru, Dec 1987 [7][11][12] vs Colaba, Mumbai, 1990, called the "first exclusive" showroom [8][9]. Likely both true in different senses; say "its first showroom in Bengaluru" and keep Colaba out, or call it "a big new showroom in Mumbai". First range Rs 350–900 [2][7][8] vs Rs 350–700 [12]; we use "about Rs 350 to 900" (already used in the Daily Call).
- [2] contains a garbled line (298 million watches by March 1987); ignore it.
- The ad tune is Mozart's; no quotes from real people are needed.
- Curriculum fit: D2's lesson codes (12.1.3, 5.2.1) are only loosely supported by the history (see D2).
