## Claims
- [supported] Kodak in 1975 was the biggest name in photography and sold the film (intro.text) — [14] gives about 85% of US camera sales and 90% of US film sales in the 1970s. "Every holiday snap in America makes you money" is rhetoric, not checked.
- [supported] Steve Sasson was a Kodak engineer and was 25 years old (lab.text) — [8] says "Sasson was 25 years old". Note: other outlets say 23 or 24, depending on whether they mean his hiring or the invention.
- [supported] The camera dates to December 1975 (lab.text, "In December") — [1] says it was first built in December 1975, and [8] says the first image was taken in December 1975.
- [supported] FIXED: lab.text now says Sasson finished the camera in December 1975, with no claim about a demo in the CEO's office — [1][7]
- [supported] 8 pounds (lab.text) — [1], [7], [8].
- [supported] 100 by 100 pixels (lab.text) — [1], [7] (0.01 megapixels).
- [supported] black-and-white (lab.text) — [7], [8].
- [supported] 23 seconds to save an image to a cassette tape (lab.text) — [1], [7], [8].
- [supported] You need a TV to see it (lab.text) — [1]: "required a separate TV to see the image".
- [supported] Kodak patented the camera (d1.options.shelve.outcome) — [1]: US 4,131,919, "Electronic Still Camera", Lloyd and Sasson, filed 20 May 1977; [7].
- [supported] FIXED: d1.options.shelve.outcome now says Kodak patented the camera, the executives who saw it weren't convinced, and no digital camera went on sale — [1][8][9]
- [supported, wording drifts] "Sasson later recalled executives asking why anyone would want to take pictures this way" (d1.options.shelve.outcome) — [9] quotes Sasson recalling that executives "were convinced that no one would ever want to look at their pictures on a television set". The objection he recalled was about *viewing on a TV*, not about *taking* pictures this way. Reword it to match.
- [supported] The film division's resistance and Kodak's choice to protect film (d1.options.shelve, the "film division cheers" framing) — broadly consistent with [10] (Kodak "did little to prepare") and [16] (the "perfect products" mentality). The specific cheering is dramatization.
- [supported] August 1981: Sony announces the Mavica, an electronic camera that records pictures on a small magnetic disk instead of film (sony1981.text, all variants) — [6]: "On August 25, 1981, Sony unveiled a prototype ... the world's first electronic still video camera", which used 2-inch disks. [6] also says it was analog, "not digital", and the text avoids calling it digital, which is good. Note that it was an unveiled prototype, not a product on sale.
- [supported] A big retailer asked Kodak what Sony's camera meant for film (d2.setup) — [10]: around the time Sony introduced its electronic camera, one of Kodak's largest retail photofinishers asked whether to worry about digital.
- [supported] Vince Barabba, Kodak's head of market intelligence, reported that digital could replace film but Kodak had roughly ten years to prepare (d2.options.study.outcome) — [10]. This is a secondary account based on Barabba's own book, *The Decision Loom*.
- [supported] FIXED: d2.options.study.outcome now ends "Kodak does little to prepare" — [10]
- [supported] It's 2001 and Kodak has just bought Ofoto, a photo-upload website (web2001.text, all variants) — [2]: bought in May 2001; [13]: purchase completed June 4, 2001; [5].
- [supported] FIXED: web2001.text default variant now says EasyShare made Kodak number two in the US while film still earned most of the money — [13]
- [supported] Film sales have peaked by 2001 (web2001.text, default variant) — [13]: worldwide consumer film sales "decreased 7% in 2001 relative to 2000", professional film sales were down 13%, and US industry film volume was "down slightly".
- [supported] Ofoto was renamed Kodak Gallery (d3.options.prints.outcome) — [2]: it became Kodak EasyShare Gallery in 2005, shortened to Kodak Gallery shortly afterward.
- [supported] The site grew to over 60 million users (d3.options.prints.outcome) — [2]: "served over 60 million users" (at its 2008 peak).
- [supported] Free accounts had to keep buying prints or risk losing their photos (d3.options.prints.outcome) — [2]: minimum purchase requirements of $5 to $20, or photos could be deleted. Precisely, the requirement was a minimum purchase, not prints specifically.

- [unsupported, likely anachronistic framing] "Phones are starting to have cameras" in 2006 (last2006.text, default variant) — [15]: the first fully integrated camera phone was released in November 2000. Search snippets (Strategy Analytics, via The Media Leader, which would not open) say camera phones outsold digital cameras worldwide in the first half of 2003. By 2006, camera phones were common, not "starting".
- [supported] In February 2007 Kodak launched EasyShare all-in-one printers (d4.options.inkjet.outcome) — [3], dated February 5, 2007: Kodak unveiled "Kodak Easyshare All-in-One Printers". - d1.rush: ok. The context claim "no home computers" in 1975 is broadly fair: the Altair 8800 kit was out, but there was no mass-market home computer until 1977.
- sony1981, shelved variant: mostly ok, but "The idea you locked away is now on the front pages, with Sony's name on it" implies that the Mavica was the same (digital) idea. It was an analog still-video camera [6]. "Front pages" is unverified.

## Other issues
- Quotes: no direct quotes of real people appear. The d1.shelve outcome paraphrases Sasson's recollection, and the paraphrase changes its meaning (see Claims).
- Year mismatch: d4 is dated 2006, and the inkjet takeaway says "In 2006 you bet on ... home printing", but the outcome and realHistory say February 2007 [3]. Align them, either by setting the d4 year to 2007 or by changing the takeaway to 2007.
- Mild anachronism: "Phones are starting to have cameras" in 2006. Camera phones existed from 2000 [15] and were mainstream by 2003 to 2004.
- The 2001 real-path scene says "Kodak is late" to digital cameras, but Kodak was number 2 in US consumer digital camera share in 2001 [13].
- Two of the file's seven listed sources are unusable for verification: Snopes (paywalled, 402) and History of Information (empty page). Consider replacing them with [8] (RPI) or [7] for Sasson, and [6] for the Mavica.

## Summary
38 claims checked: 31 supported (3 of them with wording that drifts or overstates, and 1 with contested framing), 6 unsupported, 1 contradicted.

The 3 most important fixes:
1. web2001 default text (the real-history path): drop "Kodak is late". Kodak's EasyShare cameras made it number 2 in US consumer digital camera share in 2001 [13]. Its real problem was profitability and the shift to sharing, not timing.
2. d2.study.outcome: replace "decides on a slow shift that keeps film profits as high as possible for as long as possible" with the sourced version, "Kodak did little to prepare during the window" [10]. In d1.shelve.outcome, reword the Sasson paraphrase to match his recollection, that executives doubted anyone would want to look at pictures on a television [9]. Either source the claim that "nobody is asked to turn it into a product" or remove it.
3. Fix the inkjet year mismatch (the d4 year and takeaway say 2006; the launch was February 2007), change "a fraction of rivals' prices" to "about half" ("up to 50 percent" [3]), and either source or soften "fewer and fewer of them were printing photos at all". Also change "Phones are starting to have cameras" (2006) to something like "Most new phones now have cameras".
