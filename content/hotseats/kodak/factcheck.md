## Claims
- [supported] About 9 in every 10 rolls of film sold in America were Kodak's in 1975 (nodes.intro.text) — [11]: "about 85% of camera sales and 90% of film sales in the United States in the 1970s". Note: [8] (in the file's own list) says "almost 70% of the highly lucrative US film market". Add [11] to sources so the 90% figure has a listed source.
- [supported] Kodak earned from film, chemicals and paper (nodes.intro.text; nodes.d3.setup) — [10]: Kodak "made most of its money on film, chemicals, and photo paper".
- [supported] December 1975, Kodak engineer Steve Sasson built it in a Kodak lab (nodes.lab.text) — [1], [2], [9].
- [supported] 8 pounds, 23 seconds, black-and-white, cassette tape (nodes.lab.text) — [1] (8 lb, 23 s, cassette), [2] (8 lb, 23 s, black-and-white, magnetic cassette tape).
- [supported] You need a TV to see the picture (nodes.lab.text) — [1]: "required a separate TV to see the image".
- [supported] FIXED: d1 and d2 setups now say film is where most of your money comes from; hook now says "Film is how you make your money." (nodes.d1.setup; nodes.d2.setup; hook) — Fstoppers (most of its money on film, chemicals and photo paper)
- [supported] Kodak patented the camera (nodes.d1.options[0].outcome) — [1]: US 4,131,919, Lloyd and Sasson, filed 1977.
- [supported] Managers who saw it weren't convinced; the inventor later recalled them doubting anyone would want to look at their pictures on a TV (nodes.d1.options[0].outcome; realHistory) — [12]: Sasson recalled executives were "convinced that no one would ever want to look at their pictures on a television set"; [4] and [10] record the "that's cute, but don't tell anyone" reaction. Paraphrase, not a quote. Add [12] to sources.
- [supported] No digital camera goes on sale (then) (nodes.d1.options[0].outcome) — [9]: Kodak's first pocket consumer digital cameras came in 1996; [1] notes the work stayed in the lab.
- [supported] August 1981, Sony shows the Mavica, which saves pictures on a small magnetic disk; not digital (nodes.sony1981.text[0]) — [3]: unveiled August 25, 1981 as a prototype, onto magnetic disks, "not digital".
- [supported] A big photo-printing customer asked whether to worry (nodes.d2.setup) — [4]: around when Sony introduced its electronic camera, a major retail photofinisher asked Barabba "whether they should be concerned about digital photography".
- [supported] Head of market research reported digital could replace film but Kodak had about ten years to prepare; Kodak did very little with those years (nodes.d2.options[1].outcome[1]; realHistory) — [4].
- [supported] By 2001 EasyShare made Kodak number two in US (consumer) digital cameras (nodes.web2001.text[3]) — [5]: "number two consumer market share position in the U.S., up from the number three position"; [9]: EasyShare launched 2001.
- [supported] Film still earns most of Kodak's money and film sales have started to fall, 2001 (nodes.web2001.text[3]) — [5]: Photography segment (mostly traditional film, paper, processing) $9.4bn of $13.2bn total sales; consumer film sales down 7% in 2001.
- [supported] Kodak had just bought Ofoto, a photo-upload website, 2001 (nodes.web2001.text[3]) — [5]: completed June 4, 2001; [6]; [9].
- [supported] Ofoto used to sell prints (nodes.d3.options[0]; realHistory) — [5]: Ofoto offers "top-quality prints" and Kodak saw it adding to film and paper consumption; [6]: early feature to "purchase physical silver-halide prints". [6] also lists storage, sharing and gifts.
- [supported] Renamed Kodak Gallery; more than 60 million people use it (nodes.d3.options[0].outcome) — [6]: renamed 2005; "At its peak in 2008, it served over 60 million users".
- [supported] Users had to keep buying or their photos could be deleted (nodes.d3.options[0].outcome) — [6]: minimum purchase of $5-$20, otherwise "we may delete the images stored in your account".
- [supported] By 2007 Kodak had closed factories and cut thousands of jobs; film falling fast (nodes.last2006.text[3]) — [9]: 2004, "Some 15,000 jobs are shed as it closes factories".
- [supported, slightly overstated] The photo website earns money only when people buy prints (nodes.last2006.text[3]) — [6]: revenue came from purchases (prints and also gifts/merchandise). "Mainly" would be exact.
- [supported] February 2007 Kodak showed EasyShare printers with ink it said cut costs by up to half; on sale in March (nodes.d4.options[1].outcome; realHistory) — [7]: "save consumers up to 50 percent on everything they print"; models 5100/5300 in stores "Beginning in March 2007" at Best Buy.
- [supported] The printers never became the big new business Kodak needed (nodes.d4.options[1].outcome) — [8]: inkjet investment never formed a coherent business model.
- [supported] Kodak filed for bankruptcy on January 19, 2012 (nodes.collapse.text[0]; realHistory) — [9], [10].
- [supported] April 2012 Facebook buys Instagram, 13 employees, $1 billion (nodes.collapse.text[0]) — [13] (dated 10 April 2012). Add [13] to sources.
- [supported] Kodak invented the digital camera (nodes.collapse.text[0]) — [1], [9].
- [supported] Kodak emerged in 2013 as a much smaller company (realHistory) — [14]: September 3, 2013, "a slimmed-down Kodak". Add [14] to sources.
- [supported] Fujifilm moved into healthcare, cosmetics and materials and survived (realHistory; nodes.d4.options[1].better; nodes.d4.options[2].why) — [10].
