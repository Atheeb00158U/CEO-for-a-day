## Summary
I checked 50 claims: 45 supported (5 of them with wording fixes), 3 unsupported or partly supported, and 2 contradicted.

The 1986 figures (eight plants, 7,200 people, the $210M in equipment, $228M R&D at 18%, doubled productivity, "toughest year", the 1985 DRAM exit, EPROMs kept with share gains, the ITC dumping ruling) are confirmed word for word in Intel's own 1986 Annual Report PDF on intel.com. Cite that in place of the Substack.

Most important fixes:
1. **scene1985.text[2]:** "the biggest loss in Intel's history so far" is contradicted for 1985. Intel's 1985 report shows a $1.57M net profit. The record loss was 1986's $173M.
2. **d2.setup[0]/[1]:** "For five years memory has been a small part of what you sell" is contradicted. Only DRAM was minor; memory overall was still Intel's largest revenue source in 1983. Say "DRAM".
3. **d6.setup:** "IBM, your biggest customer" is unsourced. Soften it or source it.
4. **d3.setup[1]:** "DRAM still uses a lot of your factory space" is unsupported for DRAM specifically (the source says memory overall).
5. **Wording:** in d1.options[1].outcome use "agreed to pay" (only the December 1982 agreement is confirmed). Use "DRAM" not "memory" in d2.options[1].outcome and realHistory. The yield gap is versus "US makers", not Intel specifically. Flag that $210M (1985–86, per the 1986 letter) conflicts with the $236M capital spending Intel reported for 1985 alone.
6. **Framing (optional):** Intel also cut pay in 1985, and it did pursue trade complaints, so the "pay cut" and "ask the government" alternatives aren't the opposite of what Intel did.

IBM's $250M for 12% at $40 a share, the sole-sourced 386 made in two fabs, the 4.7-year single-source run, the 9 Sept 1986 Deskpro 386 announcement and IBM's July 1987 PS/2 Model 80 all check out.
