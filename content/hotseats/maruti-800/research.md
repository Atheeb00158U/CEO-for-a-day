- 1976-03: Only 21 cars have been made [5].
- 1978: A court orders the old Maruti company dissolved [5].
- 1980: The government takes control of Maruti's assets by law [5].
- 1981-02: Maruti Udyog Ltd is founded as a government company on the same Gurgaon site [5][7].
- 1981: The target is 1,00,000 cars a year, when India's whole car market is about 35,000–40,000 a year [7].
- 1981–82: Fiat, Peugeot, Volkswagen, Renault and Nissan say no; Daihatsu is the front-runner [1][3].
- 1982-01: An India Today story names Daihatsu as the likely partner; a Suzuki director reads it [2][6].
- 1982-02: A Daihatsu telex suggests it is not serious; Suzuki telexes its interest within days [6].
- 1982-04: Talks with Suzuki begin; a letter of intent is signed [5][8][10].
- 1982-10-02: Licence and joint venture agreement signed; Suzuki takes 26%, to rise to 40% in five years [5][8].
- 1982: Suzuki pays about USD 20.8 million for its 26% [2]; Suzuki caps the project cost at USD 200 million [8].
- 1983: Plant, supplier base and dealers built in 14 months [1][3].
- 1983: About 1,20,000 bookings arrive in the first year, against a plan of 20,000 cars [8][11].
- 1983-12: The first 192 cars roll out; almost all parts are Japanese except MRF tyres and Chloride India batteries; local content is 2.76% [6].
- 1983-12-14: The Prime Minister hands the first car's keys to an Indian Airlines employee chosen by lucky draw; he paid Rs 47,500 [4][8][11].
- 1984-03: Local content still below 10% [6].
- 1984: A van with the same engine is launched [5].
- 1984–85: First full year; output beats the 20,000 target [6].
- 1985: Gypsy four-wheel-drive launched [5].
- 1986: The 100,000th car is built [5][9]; late 1986, a newer body (SB308) arrives [3][4].
- 1987-10-24: The first of 500 export cars arrive in Hungary [4].
- 1988: Gurgaon capacity reaches 1,00,000 cars a year [5].
- 1989–90: Exports reach France (1989) and the Netherlands (1990) [4]; the Maruti 1000 sedan arrives [5].
- 1985–1991: Shortages continue; capacity can't grow until 1992–93 for lack of foreign exchange [8].
- 1991: 65% of parts are made in India across all models [5].
- 1992: Suzuki's stake rises to 50% [5].
- 2023-01: The 800's engine ends production under new emission rules [1].
- 2024: Maruti Suzuki holds 41.6% of India's passenger car market [10].

## Decision points
### D1 · 1982 · Pick a partner
- Situation: Big Western makers said no; most partners wanted to bring their own staff and machines [1][7]. Daihatsu, the front-runner, looked unserious by Feb 1982 [6]. Suzuki telexed its interest [6].
- Options: (a) keep waiting for Daihatsu or a famous European name; (b) sign with Suzuki, a late but eager smaller maker; (c) take a partner that brings and runs its own plant.
- What the company did: Signed with Suzuki on 2 Oct 1982; Suzuki's president met the team himself and made the best financial offer [7]; 26% rising to 40% [8].
- What happened next: The plant, suppliers and dealers were built in 14 months [1].

### D2 · 1982 · Which car?
- Situation: Over 90% of car trips carried four or fewer people, mostly in cities [7]. The market was the big Ambassador and Padmini [9].
- Options: (a) a big family sedan; (b) a van first; (c) a small, cheap 800 cc car.
- What the company did: Built a car based on Suzuki's 1979 Alto, 796 cc, three cylinders [4]; first in India with front-wheel drive and front disc brakes [3]. A van followed in 1984 [5].
- What happened next: About 1,20,000 bookings in year one [8].

### D3 · 1983 · Make the launch date
- Situation: The deadline was December 1983 [3]; Indian suppliers were not ready [6].
- Options: (a) start with imported kits and a promise to go local; (b) delay until Indian parts are ready; (c) use existing Indian suppliers' parts as they are.
- What the company did: Started with imported kits; 2.76% local at launch, 95% promised in five years [6].
- What happened next: First 192 cars in Dec 1983; first car handed over 14 Dec 1983 [4][6].

### D4 · 1983 · Price and the queue
- Situation: 1,20,000 bookings, plan for 20,000 cars [8].
- Options: (a) raise the price to what buyers will pay; (b) keep the official price near Rs 50,000 and allot by lucky draw; (c) keep it low and borrow abroad early for more capacity.
- What the company did: Official price about Rs 50,000 [1][3] (first buyer paid Rs 47,500 [8]); allotment by lucky draw [4].
- What happened next: Buyers paid premiums, about double [1] or up to 40% [4]; waits of almost three years [4]; capacity stuck until 1992–93 for want of foreign exchange [8].

### D5 · 1984 · Local suppliers
- Situation: Local content under 10% until March 1984; 95% promised in five years [6]; hundreds of suppliers needed to upgrade quality [6].
- Options: (a) keep importing from Japan; (b) pick suppliers on quality only and train them; (c) give orders to well-connected firms.
- What the company did: The vendor team refused a politician's push and made no compromises; suppliers were pushed to upgrade [6]. Vendors were told to act as if facing competition [7].
- What happened next: Vendors got serious after 1984–85 [6]; output beat the 20,000 target in 1984–85 [6]; Bhargava says the five-year local-parts goal was met [7], though [5] gives 65% in 1991 (see Risks).

### D6 · 1987 · Sell abroad?
- Situation: 1 lakh cars built by 1986–87 [9]; long queue at home [8].
- Options: (a) cut quality checks to push more cars to the queue; (b) export to Europe to raise standards; (c) serve only India.
- What the company did: Exported from 1987 against Suzuki's and the chairman's advice, to raise quality [7]; 500 cars to Hungary, then France and the Netherlands [4]. Seat belts were fitted only once exports began [4].
- What happened next: Capacity hit 1,00,000 a year in 1988 [5]; Maruti 1000 in 1989–90 [5].

## What really happened
A government company with no car experience found a late, eager partner in Suzuki, built a small 800 cc car from a Gurgaon plant in 14 months and launched it on 14 December 1983 [1][6]. Demand far outran supply for years [8]. Maruti pushed hundreds of Indian suppliers to upgrade [6], started exporting in 1987 [4] and reached 1,00,000 cars a year by 1988 [5]. Its 800 stayed on sale until 2014, about 2.87 million built [4]; its engine lasted until 2023 [1], and the company still holds about 41.6% of India's car market [10].

- Litigation: none found central to 1981–1990.
- Conflicting numbers: launch price Rs 47,500 [8][11][13] vs Rs 48,000 [2] vs Rs 50,000 [1][3]. Queue premium "about double" [1][13] vs "up to 40%" [4]. Suzuki's first stake 25% vs 26% [8]. Local parts: "about 93% cut in five years" [7] vs 65% in 1991 [5]. Maruti 1000: 1989 or 1990 [5].
