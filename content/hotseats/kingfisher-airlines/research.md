## Decision points
### D1 · 2004–05 · What kind of airline?
- Situation: Air Deccan has started cheap flying (Aug 2003) [4] and Delhi–Mumbai fares have crashed from over ₹6,500 to ₹725–2,000 [8]. You have new A320s on order [5].
- Options: (a) launch as a low-cost airline like Air Deccan; (b) launch as a premium airline: new planes, a screen at every seat, top service in one class; (c) start small and simple, and grow only where you make money.
- What it did: (b). Launched May 2005 with brand-new A320s, video screens and "executive-class" service in a single class [5].
- What happened next: It lost ₹337 crore in the first 15 months and ₹420 crore in the next 12 [2]. It never made a yearly profit [13].

### D2 · 2007 · Buy the low-cost rival?
- Situation: Losses so far: ₹337 crore, then ₹420 crore [2]. Air Deccan, the first low-cost airline, is open to an investor [6].
- Options: (a) stay premium-only and fix your own costs first; (b) build your own low-cost brand from scratch; (c) buy a big stake in Air Deccan.
- What it did: (c). 26% of Deccan for ₹550 crore at ₹155 a share (31 May 2007) [6]; the two companies merged on 1 Apr 2008 [4], and Deccan became Kingfisher Red [4].
- What happened next: The merged airline lost ₹1,609 crore in its first full year (2008-09) [2]. Kingfisher Red was shut in Sep 2011 [1].

### D3 · 2008 · Fly abroad now?
- Situation: India's rule needs five years of domestic flying before an international licence. Kingfisher has three [7]. Air Deccan has just passed five [4][7].
- Options: (a) wait until 2010 and keep the cash for home; (b) merge Kingfisher into the older Deccan company and fly abroad in 2008; (c) put the long-haul plans on ice and use partner airlines instead.
- What it did: (b). Kingfisher's airline merged into Deccan Aviation [4]; Bangalore–London began on 3 Sep 2008 [7].
- What happened next: The five A340-500s for long routes went back [8]; London flights ended in April 2012 [12]; the A380 order was later cancelled [19].

### D4 · Late 2008 · Fuel shock and slump
- Situation: Jet fuel now costs over double its 2003 price (₹47,048/kl in March 2008) [8]. Traffic is falling. Jet and Kingfisher together lose over $2 million a day (estimate) [9].
- Options: (a) team up with your biggest rival, Jet, to share costs; (b) shrink hard: ground planes, cut routes and staff; (c) raise fares and hope rivals follow.
- What it did: (a), plus some of (b): an alliance with Jet (Oct 2008) [9], A340s returned and 32 A320s deferred [7][8].
- What happened next: Losses of ₹1,609 crore (2008-09) and ₹1,647 crore (2009-10) [2].

### D5 · 2010–11 · Ask the banks for help
- Situation: Debt of about ₹8,000 crore with 18 lenders [2]; losses every year [2].
- Options: (a) ask banks to swap some loans for shares and cut interest; (b) sell a big stake to an outside investor and give up control; (c) shrink to a small, profitable core and sell planes.
- What it did: (a). Recast approved Nov 2010 [2]. Banks took 23.4% for ₹750 crore of debt at ₹64.48 a share, about 60% above the market price; interest cut to 11%, nine years to repay; debt down to about ₹6,000 crore [10][11].
- What happened next: Loss fell to ₹1,027 crore in 2010-11 [2][13], then rose to ₹2,328 crore in 2011-12 [13]. Late pay began in Aug 2011 [2]; SBI marked the loan bad in Jan 2012 [2].

### D6 · 2012 · Staff unpaid, planes grounded
- Situation: About 4,000 staff unpaid for months [15][16]. Only 22 of 64 planes flying (Feb 2012) [2]. International flights stopped [12]. Debt about $2.5 billion [15][18]. Strike, then a lockout in October [15][16]. DGCA wants a funded revival plan [17].
- Options: (a) lock out the staff and look for a new investor; (b) pay staff first by selling group assets, then restart small; (c) hand the airline to the banks and shut down in an orderly way.
- What it did: (a). Partial lockout; flights stopped in early October [16][17].
- What happened next: DGCA suspended the licence on 20 Oct 2012 [17]; the permit lapsed at year end [18]; rights and slots were scrapped in Feb 2013 [1]. Staff got ₹311.67 crore only in Dec 2025 [33].

## Taster: the first two decisions
Use **D1 (premium or low-cost?)** and **D2 (buy Air Deccan?)**.
- Both are easy to grasp in a minute: "what kind of airline?" and "buy your rival?".
- Both are about business, not about courts or any person's conduct, so the taster carries no legal risk.
- Together they set up the whole story: a costly premium airline buys a cheap one, and losses grow. D1 → D2 flows naturally ("you are losing money; a rival is for sale").
- D3 (fly abroad) is a good third beat but needs the five-year rule explained, which is slow for a taster.

## What really happened
Kingfisher launched in May 2005 as a premium airline with new planes and seat-back screens [5]. It lost money every year [2][13]. In 2007 it bought into Air Deccan for ₹550 crore [6], merged with it in 2008 and used Deccan's older history to fly to London [4][7]. Fuel prices doubled and traffic fell in 2008 [8][9]; it lost about ₹1,600 crore in each of the next two years [2]. Banks swapped part of the debt for 23.4% of the company in 2011 [10]. Losses grew to ₹2,328 crore in 2011-12 [13]. About 4,000 staff went unpaid for up to seven months in 2012 [15][16]. The DGCA suspended its licence on 20 Oct 2012 [17], the permit lapsed at the end of 2012 [18], and its flying rights were scrapped in Feb 2013 [1].

Epilogue (labelled carefully, see Risks): banks led by SBI said they were owed more than ₹9,000 crore; a tribunal set the sum at ₹6,203.35 crore plus interest in 2017 [22]. The chairman left India on 2 Mar 2016 [20][21]. In Jan 2019 a Mumbai court declared him a fugitive economic offender [27]. UK courts approved his extradition (2018–2020), but he has not been sent back [24][26][29][30]. India's Supreme Court held him in contempt and sentenced him in 2022 [30]. The government says ₹14,131.6 crore was recovered from his assets; he says that is double what he owed [31]. Former staff got ₹311.67 crore in Dec 2025 [33]. His challenge in the Bombay High Court was still open in Feb 2026 [34][35].

## Risks
**This is the riskiest Hot Seat so far. Flag for Atheeb before drafting.** Our own rule says to avoid companies in active litigation; this one is still in court. If we go ahead, the playable part should stop at Oct 2012 (business calls only), and the epilogue should be short, dated and attributed.

Legal claims, alleged vs. found:
- **Bank dues.** Alleged: banks led by SBI said they were owed more than ₹9,000 crore [22][29]. Found: Debt Recovery Tribunal, Bengaluru, 19 Jan 2017, ordered recovery of ₹6,203.35 crore with 11.5% yearly interest from 2013 from Mallya, Kingfisher, Kingfisher Finvest and UB Holdings [22]. Safe: "Banks said Kingfisher owed them more than ₹9,000 crore. In 2017 a debt tribunal ordered ₹6,203 crore plus interest to be repaid." Never: "he stole ₹9,000 crore", "he looted the banks".
- **Recoveries.** The Finance Minister told Parliament ₹14,131.6 crore was recovered from his assets (Dec 2024) [31]. He says this is more than double the debt the tribunal fixed and he wants relief [31]. Safe: give both sides with "the government says / he says".
- **Leaving India.** Fact: he left on 2 Mar 2016, the day the banks went to the Supreme Court [20][21]. He says it was a planned trip and he was not running away [20][32]. Safe: "He left India on 2 March 2016." Avoid "fled" or "escaped" in our own voice.
- **Fugitive economic offender.** Found: special PMLA court, Mumbai, 5 Jan 2019 declared him one under the 2018 Act [27][28]. Still challenged in the Bombay High Court (Feb 2026) [34][35]. Safe: "In January 2019 a Mumbai court declared him a fugitive economic offender. He is challenging this." Don't call him "a fugitive" as a bare label.
- **Fraud and money laundering.** Alleged: CBI and ED cases (conspiracy to defraud, false representations, money laundering), including a ₹900 crore IDBI loan [23][26]. Found: only that there is a prima facie case, for extradition purposes: Westminster Magistrates' Court, 10 Dec 2018 [24], upheld by the High Court in London, 20 Apr 2020 [29]. No Indian court has convicted him of fraud. Safe: "UK courts ruled in 2018 and 2020 that there was enough of a case to send him to India for trial." Never: "he committed fraud", "he diverted the loans", "he lied to the banks" — defamatory as fact.
- **Extradition.** Found: ordered by the court (2018) and the Home Secretary (3 Feb 2019) [24][26]; appeal lost (2020) [29]. Not carried out; India's government told the Supreme Court a secret UK proceeding is pending [30]. Safe: "He has not been extradited. A separate legal matter in the UK is still open."
- **Contempt.** Found: Supreme Court of India found him guilty of contempt (2017) for moving $40 million from Diageo to his children against court orders, and on 11 Jul 2022 sentenced him to four months and a ₹2,000 fine and ordered the money back with 8% interest [30].
